\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
SelfAware~\citep{selfaware} pairs unanswerable questions (failure: hallucinating an answer) with
answerable ones (matched behavior: answering); FalseQA~\citep{falseqa} pairs false-premise questions
(failure: accepting the premise) with valid-premise ones (matched behavior: answering). Each split contains
$n{=}70$ prompts, so every reported rate is quantized at $1/70$ (${\sim}1.4$ percentage points per case),
and small gaps on either model should be read cautiously. A blind Opus judge assigns every response one
label from the ACT taxonomy \{answer, abstain, reject-premise, discuss, mixed\}. Capability cost is
teacher-forced $\Delta$-perplexity on held-out WikiText relative to the unedited model; a negative value
indicates an improvement in held-out perplexity. BLADE-G's generic-importance term is calibrated on C4,
following the standard calibrate-on-C4/evaluate-held-out pruning protocol.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Both methods are fit on the same certain/uncertain prompt contrast at the last prompt token. The
comparison therefore controls for the prompt contrast used to fit the interventions and compares the
intervention itself: a sparse static weight edit (BLADE-G) versus per-head activation steering (ITI).
BLADE-G layers are selected automatically by ELS. Qwen3-4B is evaluated with thinking off; Llama-3.2-3B
has no thinking mode. BLADE is controlled by sparsity $\rho$ and amplification $\alpha$; ITI by a dose $c$
in units of the per-head projection standard deviation. Because the two control parameters are not
commensurable, doses were swept independently on each model. On Qwen3-4B,
$\rho\in\{.002,.005,.01,.02\}$ was swept and results are reported at $\alpha{=}1.5$ for two sparsity
levels; on Llama-3.2-3B, $\rho{=}.005$ was fixed and only $\alpha$ was swept, so the Llama BLADE results
are not tuned over sparsity.

\paragraph{Qwen3-4B.}
At the higher reported ITI dose ($c{=}4$), BLADE matches the behavior at a small fraction of the
perplexity cost (Figure~\ref{fig:umc-qwen4b}). Among the reported settings, ITI $c{=}4$ reaches $20\%$
hallucination and $20\%$ false-premise acceptance, at $+14.7\%$ held-out perplexity and with SelfAware
answering reduced from $87\%$ to $67\%$. BLADE at $(\rho{=}.02,\alpha{=}1.5)$ reaches $17\%$
hallucination and $20\%$ acceptance at $+0.8\%$ perplexity, while retaining more of the matched behaviors
(SelfAware answering $71\%$ vs.\ $67\%$; true-premise answering $73\%$ vs.\ $70\%$). At the mild dose the
methods are close and neither dominates. ITI $c{=}2$ reduces false-premise acceptance substantially
($36\%\to24\%$) and hallucination modestly ($27\%\to23\%$) at $-8.4\%$ held-out perplexity; BLADE at
$(\rho{=}.002,\alpha{=}1.5)$ reaches $21\%$ hallucination and the same $24\%$ acceptance at $-0.7\%$
perplexity, with higher true-premise answering ($76\%$ vs.\ $71\%$) and tied SelfAware answering
($81\%$). This is not a Pareto improvement: ITI $c{=}2$ matches BLADE on false-premise acceptance (both
$24\%$) at lower perplexity than BLADE ($-8.4\%$ vs.\ $-0.7\%$), while BLADE offers the better
failure-versus-answering trade-off. Neither method fully preserves the matched behaviors on this model;
BLADE lowers true-premise answering from $83\%$ to $76\%$ and $73\%$ at the two sparsity levels. In the
wider sweep, $\alpha\ge2.0$ reduces the failures further than any ITI dose tried but abstains excessively
on the answerable splits, so $\alpha{=}1.5$ is the reported operating point.

\paragraph{Llama-3.2-3B.}
Llama-3.2-3B is more challenging for both methods (Figure~\ref{fig:umc-llama3b}). On SelfAware, neither
method separates the failure from the matched behavior. BLADE reduces unanswerable hallucination from
$47\%$ to $33\%$ ($\alpha{=}1.75$) and $37\%$ ($\alpha{=}2.0$), but answering on the answerable split
falls from $73\%$ to $40\%$ at both settings; ITI shows the same trade, with hallucination $39\%$ and
$29\%$ against answering $65\%$ and $44\%$ at $c{=}1$ and $c{=}2$. ITI's perplexity cost rises steeply
on this model, from $+7.9\%$ ($c{=}1$) to $+45.8\%$ ($c{=}2$), whereas BLADE costs $+1.8\%$
($\alpha{=}1.75$) and $+3.5\%$ ($\alpha{=}2.0$). On FalseQA, the methods separate clearly. BLADE at
$\alpha{=}2.0$ reduces false-premise acceptance from $29\%$ to $13\%$ while preserving true-premise
answering ($71\%$, equal to base); ITI reaches only $21\%$ acceptance at $c{=}2$ and lowers true-premise
answering to $55\%$. At $c{=}1$, ITI slightly increases false-premise acceptance above base
($29\%\to31\%$) at $+7.9\%$ perplexity. FalseQA is therefore the axis on which the interventions separate
consistently on this model.

\paragraph{Cross-model summary.}
The trade-offs differ across models. On Qwen3-4B, BLADE reaches the failure rates of the higher reported
ITI dose ($c{=}4$) at a small fraction of its perplexity cost, and at the mild dose the two methods split
the objectives: ITI $c{=}2$ matches BLADE on false-premise acceptance at lower perplexity, while BLADE
retains more of the matched behaviors. On Llama-3.2-3B, among the evaluated settings, SelfAware admits no
clean operating point for either method, because every setting that lowers hallucination also
substantially reduces answering; FalseQA separates the methods, with BLADE at $\alpha{=}2.0$ reaching the
lowest false-premise acceptance observed while leaving true-premise answering at its base level, and ITI
reaching a smaller reduction only with a large perplexity increase and a substantial reduction in
answering. Across both models, the sparse static edit keeps held-out perplexity within a few percent of
the unedited model at every reported setting, whereas the per-head activation dose incurs a steep,
model-dependent perplexity cost at the doses that reduce the failures, and at low dose can increase the
failure, as on Llama-3.2-3B at $c{=}1$. Neither method preserves the matched behaviors in general: BLADE
lowers true-premise answering on Qwen3-4B and answering on the answerable split on Llama-3.2-3B, and full
preservation is observed only for Llama-3.2-3B FalseQA at $\alpha{=}2.0$.
