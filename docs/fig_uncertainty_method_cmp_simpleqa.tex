\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on three closed-book benchmarks that probe complementary facets of
epistemic (un)certainty. \textbf{SelfAware}~\cite{selfaware} pairs answerable questions with
\emph{unanswerable} ones---open scientific unknowns, under-specified or subjective queries with no known
answer; a calibrated model answers the former and abstains on the latter, so we report the hallucination
rate on the unanswerable half and the answer rate on the answerable half.
\textbf{FalseQA}~\cite{falseqa} pairs questions built on a \emph{false premise} (a presupposition that is
factually wrong) with matched valid-premise questions; a calibrated model rejects or corrects the false
premise rather than answering as if it held, so we report false-premise acceptance and true-premise
answering. \textbf{SimpleQA}~\cite{simpleqa} is a factuality benchmark of short fact-seeking questions,
each with a single known gold answer; unlike the first two it is answer-graded, so each response is
bucketed as \emph{correct}, \emph{incorrect} (an answer that contradicts the gold), or
\emph{not attempted} (an abstention---including responses that deny the entity exists). We report the
incorrect rate and the attempted accuracy (correct-given-attempted,
$\mathrm{correct}/(\mathrm{correct}+\mathrm{incorrect})$). SelfAware/FalseQA rates use $n\!=\!70$ per
cell for base and ITI and $n\!=\!40$ for BLADE; SimpleQA uses $n\!=\!400$, greedy generation capped at
$128$ tokens, graded by a blind LLM with a SimpleQA-style rubric (not the paper's reference grader).

Both interventions are fit on the \emph{same} certain/uncertain prompt contrast at the last prompt
token, so the comparison isolates the mechanism---a sparse, static weight edit versus per-head
activation steering---rather than the direction source. We report a single BLADE operating point
($\rho\!=\!0.005$, $\alpha\!=\!2.5$; BLADE-G, thinking-off, layers auto-selected to
$L^\star\!=\![23,31,18,2]$ and held fixed across all benchmarks) and ITI at steering strength
$c\!\in\!\{4,6\}$ (in units of per-head projection std; $c$ is ITI's dose, distinct from BLADE's
amplification $\alpha$).

\paragraph{SelfAware and FalseQA.} ITI reduces SelfAware unanswerable hallucination only at a strength
that is catastrophic for capability: it leaves this rate at the base $20\%$ at $c\!=\!4$ and lowers it
to $16\%$ only at $c\!=\!6$, where C4 teacher-forced perplexity rises by $57.3\%$ (panel~a) and
answering on SelfAware answerable questions collapses to $47\%$ (panel~c). BLADE instead lowers
unanswerable hallucination to $10\%$ (panel~b; $4/40$ items) while holding perplexity essentially flat
($-0.3\%$) and preserving answering at $85\%$ (base $87\%$). On FalseQA false-premise acceptance
(panel~d) BLADE reaches $15\%$ (base $23\%$), comparable to ITI $c\!=\!4$ ($14\%$, but at $+17.5\%$
perplexity) and beaten only by the capability-destroying $c\!=\!6$ ($9\%$); BLADE retains most
true-premise answering ($75\%$ vs.\ base $84\%$; panel~e).

\paragraph{SimpleQA.} On the answer-graded benchmark the picture is different, and worth stating plainly
(panels~f,g; Table~\ref{tab:simpleqa}). The base model is badly miscalibrated---it attempts almost every
question and is wrong $89\%$ of the time (only $7\%$ abstentions). At its tuned operating point BLADE
transfers only weakly here: it lowers incorrect merely to $83\%$ and leaves attempted accuracy at
$3.8\%$ (base $4.0\%$), i.e.\ essentially no calibration gain on external factual questions. ITI shifts
more mass out of the incorrect bucket ($74\%$ at $c\!=\!4$, $63\%$ at $c\!=\!6$), but at a large
capability cost ($+17.5\%$ / $+57.3\%$ perplexity), and part of $c\!=\!6$'s abstentions are degenerate
repetition rather than genuine declining. Two caveats temper all of these numbers. First, overall
correctness stays near the floor for every condition (Table~\ref{tab:simpleqa}): Qwen3-8B knows the
answer to very few SimpleQA questions, so any movement is a reshuffling between incorrect and
not-attempted rather than added correct answers, and the attempted-accuracy and F-score gaps are within
noise. Second, the not-attempted bucket here is dominated by \emph{denials of existence} (``there is no
record of\dots'') rather than ``I don't know''; on SimpleQA's real-entity questions such a denial is
itself a factual error that the rubric nonetheless scores as not-attempted---so ``not attempted'' should
be read as ``did not commit to the gold,'' not as well-calibrated abstention. Net: the edit tuned for
SelfAware/FalseQA does not confer a SimpleQA calibration benefit at this scale.
