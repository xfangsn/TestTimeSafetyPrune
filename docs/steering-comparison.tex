\subsubsection{Boosting Refusal}
\label{sec:exp-refusal-amplify}
Since BLADE localizes weights that modulate a behavior, its mask can also be used to \emph{strengthen} that behavior by multiplying the selected weights by $\alpha>1$. We evaluate whether this weight-level intervention strengthens refusal behaviors effectively by comparing with activation steering techniques on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, using implementations that follow the original intervention designs: Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)~\cite{rimsky2024caa}, additive steering with the refusal direction of~\cite{arditi2024refusal} (RefDir; we use their direction additively in this section, as their published intervention is directional \emph{ablation} rather than strengthening), and Activation Addition (ActAdd)~\cite{turner2024actadd}.

\paragraph{Settings}
All hyperparameters are selected exclusively on an in-distribution refusal validation set and are then frozen. For steering methods, we select the intervention layer and steering coefficient. For BLADE, we consider two pre-specified sparsity levels, $\rho$, and use ELS to select the effective layers under a perplexity budget $\beta$; the reweighting factor $\alpha$ is selected separately. Selection is based on three validation metrics: refusal under attack on \texttt{harmful\_val}, over-refusal on held-out benign prompts, and perplexity on C4.
We evaluate each frozen configuration once on three held-out test sets: HarmBench~\cite{mazeika2024harmbench} under a prefill attack, which measures out-of-distribution (OOD) refusal; the 250 safe prompts from XSTest~\cite{rottger2024xstest}, which measure benign over-refusal; and WikiText, which measures the change in perplexity. None of these test sets is used for method selection.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:judge-disagreement}, keyword matching may fail to recognize deflective responses in challenging tasks, especially OOD queries. Therefore, we evaluate refusal and over-refusal using two judges: a string matcher calibrated against the XSTest reference labels, with a false-positive rate of $0.4\%$, and an independent LLM judge, Kimi~K3 \cite{kimiteam2026kimik3}, via its official API. We compare two BLADE masks: a sparse best-first ELS mask ($\rho{=}0.002$, $\beta{=}5\%$) and a denser removal-default mask ($\rho{=}0.005$, $\beta{=}5\%$). Both use the validation-selected amplification factor $\alpha{=}1.5$. For each steering baseline, we report results at both its validation-selected coefficient and the next larger coefficient in its predefined search grid; test data are never used to select these coefficients. RefDir is the only baseline whose next larger coefficient achieves an OOD refusal rate comparable to that of sparse BLADE, enabling a cost comparison at matched safety levels.

\paragraph{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:refusal-amplify} supports three findings.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] \emph{Selectivity depends on the mask.}
    With the sparse ELS mask ($\rho{=}0.002$) and $\alpha{=}1.5$, OOD refusal rises from
    $9.0\%/25.3\%$ (base; string-match/LLM) to $33.0\%/46.0\%$, XSTest over-refusal rises by only
    $1.6$--$4.0$ points (from $7.6\%/9.6\%$ to $11.6\%/11.2\%$), and WikiText perplexity increases
    $0.27\%$. At the same $\alpha$, the denser removal-default mask ($\rho{=}0.005$) reaches
    $48.5\%/76.0\%$ OOD refusal but over-refuses $25.6\%/27.6\%$ of safe prompts. A mask chosen as
    the default for \emph{removal} is thus not the preferred mask for selective \emph{amplification}.
  \item[(ii)] \emph{Sparse BLADE reaches high OOD refusal at far lower cost.}
    Sparse BLADE ($33.0\%/46.0\%$) yields $1.7$--$2.1\times$ the OOD refusal of CAA ($15.5\%/25.3\%$)
    and ActAdd ($17.5\%/27.3\%$) at their validation-selected coefficients, at roughly one-tenth or
    less of their WikiText perplexity increase ($0.27\%$ vs.\ $2.73\%$/$3.20\%$). At the larger
    coefficients CAA ($16.5\%/33.3\%$) and ActAdd ($13.5\%/31.3\%$) stay below sparse BLADE under
    both judges while their perplexity rises to $6.4\%$ and $16.8\%$. Only RefDir at $c{=}0.5$
    exceeds sparse BLADE under both judges ($39.0\%/48.7\%$), but at $22.8\%$ XSTest over-refusal
    --- about twice BLADE's $11.6\%/11.2\%$ --- and an $8.5\%$ perplexity increase, $\sim\!30\times$
    BLADE's.
  \item[(iii)] \emph{The trade-off is consistent across judges.}
    Both reported judges place sparse BLADE above every CAA and ActAdd configuration on OOD refusal
    and identify the denser BLADE mask as the most over-refusing configuration shown. RefDir at
    $c{=}0.5$ is the only exception to a uniform OOD ranking --- it exceeds sparse BLADE by $6.0$
    points (string match) and $2.7$ points (Kimi~K3), at the higher costs noted above. The LLM
    judge credits hedged and deflecting outputs, lifting high-coefficient CAA and ActAdd to
    $33.3\%$/$31.3\%$, still below sparse BLADE's $46.0\%$.
\end{enumerate}
